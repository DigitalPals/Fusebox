# The dashboard

Everything is served from the binary at `/`, with no external requests.

Account details and drawers show each reported usage window separately, alongside spending limits, credit balances, feature allowances, and optional paid usage. Codex Business spending-only responses are usable quota readings: a spent individual cap pauses the account even when no five-hour or weekly window exists. A missing reset time stays unknown, and a partial response cannot erase exhaustion learned earlier. Numeric strings and explicit zero balances are preserved; provider credits are not treated as dollars.

Meta subscriptions report a 5-hour window and a weekly window too. Meta has no usage endpoint, so Fusebox reads them from the subscription frame each response streams, and keeps the last reading across restarts. An idle account is also checked through Meta's key mint, which is free and returns the same key; it is used when the mint carries the usage, along with its tier name as the plan.

Claude model limits, OAuth-app limits, and optional extra usage are kept distinct. Code-review/Cowork allowances and extra credits do not block ordinary chat or grant permission to spend on overages. Additional Codex limits with explicit model IDs (or a model ID as their metered-feature identifier) apply only to that model. Antigravity quota groups preserve independent bucket reset times; the provider's Gemini and Claude/GPT groups, or explicit model IDs, determine which models they constrain. Unknown groups remain informational. Antigravity reports a 5-hour and a weekly limit for each group, not for the account, so the lists draw the Gemini group's as the account's own **5H** and **WK** meters and the Claude and GPT group's as a child row (**↳ Claude & GPT**) with a meter for each; the account page names all four windows. Antigravity accounts also show their Google AI plan (**Ultra**, **Pro**, or **Free tier**) and their **Google AI credits** balance, read from Google's `loadCodeAssist` every six hours; credits are only shown, never spent. Google labels each quota bucket with an ID: `gemini-argon-` and `gemini-carbon-` buckets limit only those models (and take them out of the general Gemini one), other `gemini-` buckets the rest of Gemini, `3p-` buckets Claude and GPT. A bucket Google marks disabled is shown as such and limits nothing. With two or more Antigravity accounts, **Combined weekly quota** shows them together (Gemini as the account's week, Claude & GPT under it), and pace warnings and the **Used by** split cover them too; each group counts only its own models. When Google suspends an Antigravity account for a terms-of-service violation, or asks you to verify it, the account shows **Suspended** or **Verify** with Google's appeal or verify link, and requests skip it. A suspension stays until you clear the account's error (after appealing) or sign in again; Fusebox checks a verification every 10 minutes with a free `loadCodeAssist` call and puts the account back once Google no longer asks. While Google holds an account, its error stays Google's (other failures don't replace it, and **Clear all** cooldowns brings it back), the faults menu and notifications say **Suspended by Google** or **Google asks to verify this account**, and the combined weekly quota doesn't count on it. The hold survives a restart. Smart quota routing also accounts for the tightest applicable spending/model cap.

The **Models** tab groups every model id by family, shows aliases and model groups, and lists the order in which accounts would take a new session, with the next one marked. **Try a model** sends a prompt through routing like any request and shows which account answered and how fast. Its **Inspect a request** mode takes a request body as a client would send it (Responses, Chat Completions, Claude Messages or Gemini, with optional headers and a named client) and shows what Fusebox would send for it without sending anything: the model it becomes after `model-map`, model groups and `background-model`, the route and the account a new session would use, whether the body passes through or is translated, the upstream URL, headers and body with credentials and account ids redacted, the generation controls a translation drops, or the refusal the client would get. An inspection uses no quota and records no usage, log or session. The same is available as management-authenticated `POST /api/inspect` with `{"body": …, "format"?, "headers"?, "client"?, "model"?, "stream"?}`.

**Who used the quota.** Under each meter, an account's page shows which of your named clients and shared keys used the current window and the last one, in points of the window, plus what was used outside Fusebox (claude.ai, the desktop app). Its coding sessions show their share of the current windows. The Usage page's **Top clients** ranks every client by name in the selected range, with its share of the estimated cost, its tokens and the models it used. Tokens and models are what Fusebox recorded; the points of a window are estimates: each rise in the provider's reported use is split over the attempts that finished since the previous rise, by their API list price ([how](usage-costs.md#who-used-a-subscription-window)).

Each quota meter has a thin mark where usage would be if spread evenly over the window. Each meter shows a countdown to the provider-reported reset, with the full reset time on hover. Claude subscriptions show their plan (Ultra, Max, Pro), as Claude names it; Max 20x and Max 5x were renamed Ultra and Max on 30 October 2026. Verified Ultra accounts, and ChatGPT Pro accounts while OpenAI keeps Pro free of it, have no five-hour limit: Fusebox reads this from each usage reading, not the plan name. The finding is kept in `.quota.state` in the auth directory, so a restart doesn't bring the meter back while the usage endpoint is busy; a reported five-hour window clears it. When a provider's usage endpoint answers 429, the quota check, banked resets and the window starter all wait for its Retry-After (one to 30 minutes) before asking again. The account lists show only their weekly meter, with **no 5h limit** under the account name; the account page explains it, and the window starter is hidden and leaves them alone. Codex Pro subscriptions show **Pro 100**, **Pro 200** or **Pro 500** when ChatGPT's active subscription identifies the tier; these names refer to the USD list-price tiers, not the account's local-currency bill or a usage multiplier. Plan details are checked every six hours without using model tokens. ChatGPT accounts also show when the subscription charges next (**Ends** when cancelled), with a notification two days before (`notifications.renewals`), a day before (`notifications.renewals-day-before`, off by default), or both. Claude and Meta give no billing date to signed-in tools, so their account page has **Set billing date** in the sign-in card: enter the day of the last or next charge and Fusebox counts on from it every month (or every year, for Claude Pro, the only one of these plans sold yearly), with the same reminder. **Cancelled** makes it **Ends** on the first billing day still to come. The day is kept as `billing` in the account's credential file, with the plan it was set on: after a plan change, which can move the billing day, it shows as an estimate to check and sends no reminder until it is saved again. The overview shows **Renews tomorrow** (or **Ends**) under a subscription's status in the last three days. If ChatGPT does not expose a recognized active subscription, the broad usage plan (such as Pro) is shown. The overview's **Combined weekly quota** shows each provider's subscriptions together as one weekly row, drawn like an account's: the current combined quota in the Used/Remaining mode, the even-pace mark and a countdown to the next account's reset. Claude accounts count by their published weekly allowance (Pro 1, Max 5, Ultra 10) when every plan is known and equally otherwise. A weekly limit on one model, such as Claude's Fable limit (up to half the weekly allowance), shows as a child row (**↳ Fable**) under its subscription and under the combined row, coloured as a whole when it runs low. Fusebox reads it whether or not Claude marks it as the binding limit (`is_active`), and Fable requests also weigh it when choosing an account. Beside the bar, a forecast of the next seven days says whether the quota will last. It uses recent provider-reported quota consumption, the average of the last day's rate and the last week's, spends the account resetting first, and refills each account only at its scheduled reset; unused quota expires. **Expected to last**, **Low reserve** (below 10%) or **Quota gap expected** describes the estimate; a gap names the projected depletion and recovery times. New accounts show **Learning usage** until six hours of continuous readings are available. The week of history survives resets and restarts; missing quota/reset data, expired resets awaiting confirmation, stale readings or a required sign-in make the forecast unavailable. This forecasts weekly capacity assuming requests can move between accounts, not uninterrupted service under five-hour/model limits. Banked resets are not assumed. See [the forecast calculation](weekly-outlook.md). The Accounts page lines up every subscription's next weekly reset over seven days, and each account page sets whether it takes new sessions **First**, **Normal** or **Last**.

Sharing a screenshot or your screen? The eye button in the top bar (or <kbd>.</kbd>) hides every email and API key on the page, and copy buttons still copy the real values.

Choose **Used** or **Remaining** beside the quota meters (or press <kbd>U</kbd>); your browser remembers it. Each meter has 20 segments of 5%: they light up off-white while there's room, amber from 75% used and red from 95%.

<kbd>⌘K</kbd> / <kbd>Ctrl K</kbd> or <kbd>/</kbd> opens a command palette for accounts, models and actions such as connecting an account, clearing cooldowns, switching between light, dark and the system's theme, or switching between English and Chinese (the dashboard follows the browser's language until you pick one). The faults button in the top bar lists what has tripped: expired sign-ins, used-up limits, rate limits and runs of failed requests. Other tools can read the same list from the management API ([details](notifications.md#reading-faults-from-other-tools)).

**Clear a saved error.** Open an account's drawer or detail page and click **Clear error** beside **Last error**. This dismisses the displayed occurrence and updates other open dashboards. Cooldowns, provider quota, counters and request history remain in place. A new failure appears again; a newer error arriving while you clear requires a fresh review. Clearing sends no provider request. Claude's third-party extra-usage error also links to its usage settings.

**Install it as an app, with notifications.** Over HTTPS (or on `localhost`) the dashboard installs like an app: from Chrome or Edge's install button, Safari's **Add to Dock**, or **Add to Home Screen** on a phone. It opens in its own window, shows the number of faults on its icon, and can send push notifications when a sign-in expires or every account of a provider is used up (and when one is back). Turn them on per device under **Config, Notifications** and choose the events there. See [notifications](notifications.md).

**Banked resets** (off by default). Claude and ChatGPT sometimes give subscribers saved resets that clear a usage limit early. Turn on `banked-resets` (Config, Connections) and subscriptions that have some show “↻ 2 resets banked” under their status; click it to see expiry dates and spend one with confirmation. Smart quota balancing prefers accounts with a reset expiring within seven days. Automatic use is separately opt-in under Config, Routing: redeem at 95% weekly use, or one hour before expiry regardless of usage, including 0%. It shares the crash-safe reset journal. See [banked resets](banked-resets.md).

**Start idle usage windows** (off by default). Under Config, Routing, choose Claude subscription accounts and start their inactive five-hour windows automatically, always or during selected working hours. Each starter sends one small model request and consumes a little subscription quota; it brings the next reset forward without increasing weekly allowance. Supported accounts also have a manual **Start now** action. Active windows, real work, exhausted limits, accounts without a five-hour limit (verified Ultra) and uncertain provider data are skipped. Attempts survive restarts and share locking with banked resets. See [window starters](window-starters.md).
